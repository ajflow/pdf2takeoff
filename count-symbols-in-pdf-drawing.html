How to auto-count fixtures on a drawing

  1. Open the drawing. Drag any PDF floor plan into pdf2takeoff.
  2. Click one symbol. Press S (Scan) and click directly on one instance of the symbol: a downlight, a power point, a smoke alarm.
  3. Every match gets counted. The scan finds all instances of that symbol on the sheet and places a numbered marker on each, in reading order.
  4. Group by spec name. Rename the group (e.g. Downlight DL-100) and scan the next symbol type; the list keeps one total per group.
  5. Export. Take the counts to CSV or Excel, print the quantity report, or save the count markers into the PDF itself.

What it's good at, honestly

The scan is classic computer vision: it learns the shape of the symbol you clicked and matches every instance by size, aspect, fill and shape signature. That makes it fast, private and dependable for isolated symbols in vector CAD plots: lighting layouts, power and data schedules, fire services, ceiling grids. Symbols that touch wall lines, or dense scanned sheets, are harder: those are the target of the ML detector being trained on an open data pipeline, which drops into the same scan without changing how you use it.

Why count with a tool at all?

Fixture quantities are the classic takeoff error: 63 downlights on the lighting layout, 58 in the estimate, and nobody finds out until the electrician's variation arrives. A click-per-sheet count you can repeat on every revision and cross-check against the schedule turns that risk into a 30-second check. Pair it with the revision overlay compare to see exactly which fixtures moved between revisions before you recount.

FAQ

Is it really automatic?

One click teaches the scan what to count; every match on the page is found and marked automatically after that.

Does it work on scanned drawings?

Clear scans of distinct symbols usually work. Dense or low-contrast scans are where the ML detector (in training) will take over.

Can I count symbols on all pages?

Yes: scan each sheet, and the markups list totals every label across the whole set for the Excel export.

Do the count markers show in Bluebeam?

Yes. Save into the PDF and the count markers become standard PDF annotations that open in Bluebeam Revu and Acrobat.
