# Recent research sharpens pdf2takeoff's five open fixes

Yes: a substantial body of 2024-2026 research bears on automated takeoff from PDF drawings. Almost none of it solves pdf2takeoff's problem end to end, though. Its value lies in two places. First, a wave of new benchmarks shows frontier vision-language models still fail at exactly the operations takeoff depends on. They manage roughly **54% exact match on door and window counts**, about **30% on reading scales into numbers**, and **52.65% on whole-sheet questions**. That is stronger and more recent support for the repo's "agent plans, deterministic tools produce every number" design than the 65-paper review carried. Second, small engineering methods from neighbouring fields map closely onto today's open bugs:

- **Tile-edge double counting:** sliced-inference tooling (SAHI) merges cross-tile boxes using intersection over the smaller box (IoS).
- **Dynamic Fill:** robotics room segmentation and 2013-era wall-detection rules address door bridging and furniture-line closing.
- **Upside-down vertical dimensions:** mechanical-drawing OCR (eDOCr2) and the aligned-dimension drafting convention fix the problem without a model.
- **Scale detection:** RANSAC-style consensus over several scale sources catches misreads.

Most recommended fixes are deterministic and take hours to days. On the learned side, three findings change the existing patch plan. The CC BY 4.0 ResPlan dataset removes the licence blocker on Patch 2 Tier B. An official Apache-2.0 PaddleOCR.js SDK replaces Patch 3's planned hand-built ONNX port. An unreviewed but directly relevant floor-plan experiment argues that Patch 5 should combine pixel correlation (NCC) with DINOv2 rather than use DINOv2 alone. The new revision-compare evidence also argues against the plan's "no patch proposed" stance. The biggest caveat is that no peer-reviewed study measures seam double counting on drawings, furniture-aware gap closing or 180° confusion on short numeric strings. Every recommendation below still needs validating on the repo's own sheets.

> **How to read this report.** The sandbox's network policy blocked arXiv and most paper hosts (MDPI, CVF open access, Hugging Face, Semantic Scholar) for all five research threads. **Most paper figures below therefore come from abstracts, HTML snippets and search-engine extraction, not from full texts.** Table values in particular should be checked against the PDFs before anyone quotes them externally. Some sources were read directly: GitHub repositories (SAHI, eDOCr2, PlanSpot, PlanMetric, AEC-Bench, AECV-Bench), the npm package for PaddleOCR.js, the pdf.js source and the pdf2takeoff code. Vendor and vendor-authored claims are labelled as such throughout. The recommendations are the researchers' inferences. None has been run on pdf2takeoff's drawings, and the arithmetic about tiles uses the spec's example values, not a shipped model manifest. The prior review ([ai-literature-review.md](../docs/research/ai-literature-review.md)) and patch plan ([patch-plan.md](../docs/research/patch-plan.md)) are background; this report covers only what is new or what changes them.

## Frontier models still miss the numbers takeoff runs on

The clearest new evidence is that 2026 drawing benchmarks agree on where VLMs fail. **AECV-Bench** (AECFoundry, preprint) tests ten current models on 120 floor plans. Text tasks such as OCR reach about **0.95 accuracy**, while counting doors and windows lags at **0.40-0.55**, with large proportional errors ([arXiv 2601.04819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04819); [GitHub](https://github.com/AECFoundry/AECV-Bench)). Its project leaderboard lists a top counting score of **54.3% exact match and 15.3% MAPE**, with the best open model at 47%. That leaderboard figure is unverified against the paper ([aecv-bench.com](https://aecv-bench.com/)). **MeasureBench** (CVPR 2026, peer-reviewed) finds the best of 17 VLMs reads real instrument scales at only **about 30%**. The models recognise units above 90% of the time but "falter on mapping scales to numeric values" ([CVPR 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/papers/Lin_Do_Vision-Language_Models_Measure_Up_Benchmarking_Visual_Measurement_Reading_with_CVPR_2026_paper.pdf)). That is the same failure as misreading a scale bar or a dimension. **LED-BENCH** (October 2026, preprint) tops out at **52.65%** on large-format engineering sheets, and every model degrades when evidence spans a wider region of the sheet ([arXiv 2610.19571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19571)). Counting failure is general, not AEC-specific. On CountQA the best model reaches **42.9%** ([arXiv 2508.06585](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06585)). On HoloCount's high-density subset a 27B model reaches **20%** against over 80% elsewhere ([arXiv 2607.06420](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.06420v1)). Dense electrical and reflected-ceiling sheets are exactly that high-density case.

The agent studies point the same way, but most come from vendors. **AEC-Bench** is a vendor-authored preprint from Nomic. It found that adding a structured-parse tool to Claude Code and Codex harnesses raised retrieval-heavy drawing tasks by **18.75-32.2%**, but helped little on visually grounded tasks ([arXiv 2603.29199](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.29199)). Its telemetry is revealing. **77% of agent trajectories used `pdftotext`** as the main extraction channel, and heavy rasterization "does not lead to better performance on visually grounded tasks" ([Nomic blog](https://www.nomic.ai/news/aec-bench-a-multimodal-benchmark-for-agentic-systems-in-architecture-engineering-and-construction)). **Handoff-H1** is also a vendor-authored preprint; the company built both the system and the benchmark. Its orchestrated pipeline (computer-vision models, tool-using agents, a project knowledge base) scores **81.6 composite** on real blueprint takeoffs. Frontier models driving generic tools on the same PDFs score **34.7-61.4**. Professional estimators still beat H1 on quantity precision: **87.9 vs 78.8** for quantities within ±25% ([arXiv 2608.15032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15032)). The only independent, peer-reviewed field test found is an ASC 2026 case study of Togal.AI. It reports **near-perfect count items but systematic underestimation of area items**, with exterior finishes worst ([EasyChair](https://easychair.org/publications/paper/pkrRp)). Commercial accuracy figures in the 94.5-99% range remain **vendor claims** with undisclosed methods. Some describe human-reviewed deliverables rather than raw AI output ([Bildrix analysis](https://bildrix.com/research/ai-takeoff-accuracy)).

Three implications follow for the open problems. First, the shipped `read_text` tool (Patch 4) matches what agents already do on their own, which is to read the text layer first. That makes its wrong positions for vertical words (covered below) more urgent than a cosmetic bug. An agent pairing a vertical "3600" with the wrong dimension line will transcribe a wrong number with full confidence. Second, the BIM-agent literature names unit handling and value faithfulness, not data format, as the bottleneck ([Zenodo, IFC-Bench representations](https://zenodo.org/records/22679659)). Every numeric MCP result should therefore carry its unit, scale source and source bounding box. Third, the ASC area finding suggests area tools should report their boundary convention and gap-closing parameters with each result. Part of what looks like "AI underestimation" may be a convention mismatch, though the paper does not attribute a cause.

Revision comparison is where the new evidence most clearly overturns the patch plan. The plan proposed no patch because the literature was thin. That is still true for learned methods: no peer-reviewed AEC change-detection method or revision dataset turned up. However, VLMs are now shown to be poor at finding changes. On DiffSpot the best of 13 models finds **40.7% of true changes**, under 23% on the hard tier ([arXiv 2605.29615](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29615)). On VDiff-Bench models answer "no difference" **up to 80.9%** of the time on low-level questions ([Papers with Code](https://paperswithcode.co/paper/2609.06245)). The one peer-reviewed drawing comparator, **Drawing-Checker** (Procedia CIRP 2026), works only after deterministic view matching. It reports F1 0.950 and a 1.5% hallucination rate on mechanical drawings, figures taken from a secondary table ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2212827126008085); [ResearchGate table](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Summary-of-Visual-Reasoning-Experiments_tbl1_406593809)). A practitioner recipe diffs vector PDF primitives directly into exact, moved, fuzzy, added and modified, and groups the results into regions, because one object exports as many paths ([Boardflare](https://www.boardflare.com/templates/pdf-drawing-diff/)). AutoCAD's DWG Compare groups changes into proximity "change sets" in the same way ([Autodesk Help](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2021/ENU/AutoCAD-LT/files/GUID-2D69E78D-5C82-464F-B864-CD29D5720EB9.htm)).

Together these support a tiered `compare_revisions`:

1. Register the two sheets.
2. Diff vector primitives where both PDFs are vector.
3. Diff text-layer tokens, so a changed dimension is reported as a changed value.
4. Fall back to a raster overlay for scans.
5. Optionally let a VLM caption grouped regions, but never ask it to find changes.

The most valuable output would be a **quantity delta**: re-run saved count and measure items on the new revision and report the differences. No paper addresses this, so it is a differentiator rather than a catch-up item. Bluebeam Max now pairs Revu with Claude over MCP and adds a "Smart Overlay". Its published AI scope is markup automation over human-drawn data, and its overlay method is undisclosed (**vendor claim**) ([Bluebeam press release](https://press.bluebeam.com/2026/05/bluebeam-max-launches-globally-bringing-ai-powered-productivity-to-aec-teams-everywhere/)).

## Tile seams need IoS merging and overlap wider than any symbol

The tile-edge double count has a well-understood mechanism, even though no paper measures it on drawings. pdf2takeoff runs one per-class greedy IoU-NMS over all windows. It caps the result at `MAX_DET = 500` across all classes before the clicked class is chosen, and throws when a sheet needs more than `MAX_WINDOWS = 512` windows ([scan-onnx.ts](../src/engine/scan-onnx.ts); [detector-tiling.md](../specs/detector-tiling.md)). The reference sliced-inference library behaves differently. **SAHI** defaults to greedy non-maximum *merging* using **intersection over the smaller box (IoS) at 0.5**, per class, with 20% overlap and an extra full-image pass ([SAHI predict.py](https://github.com/obss/sahi/blob/main/sahi/predict.py); [SAHI utils.py](https://github.com/obss/sahi/blob/main/sahi/postprocess/utils.py)). IoS is the metric that matters at seams. Suppose one tile sees 40% of a symbol and the next sees all of it. Their IoU is about 0.4, below typical NMS thresholds, so both boxes survive. Their IoS is 1.0, so they merge. The SAHI maintainer's advice that merging beats NMS "in practical use cases" is informal guidance, not a measured result ([SAHI Discussion #1086](https://github.com/obss/sahi/discussions/1086)). The one 2026 paper aimed squarely at objects cut by tile seams studies PCB defects, not drawings (TA-TM, preprint, abstract only). It adds tile-adjacency-aware rescoring before global NMS and finds that **128 px overlap "lifts boundary-zone recall substantially"** ([arXiv 2605.24726](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24726)). The same paper gives the starkest evidence yet for the repo's existing training-scale rule. A detector trained on full images collapses to **mAP@50 = 0.01** under tile inference, against **0.72-0.94** when trained on tile-scale crops.

Arithmetic on the spec's own numbers shows why merging alone cannot fix pdf2takeoff's case. A 9 mm symbol is about **76 raster px** at 3 px/pt, but the spec's example overlap is **32 px**. A symbol straddling a seam can therefore end up split into two complementary halves with IoS near zero, and no tile ever sees it whole. The researchers' recommended order is:

1. Set the overlap to at least the largest symbol plus margin, about 96 px for 9 mm.
2. Drop any detection touching an interior window edge. Once step 1 holds, a neighbouring window always contains the whole symbol, so this is safe.
3. Merge only pairs from *different* windows using IoS 0.5, keeping the existing IoU test for pairs from the same window so rows of adjacent downlights are not over-merged.

Practitioner code drops inner-edge boxes in exactly this way ([practitioner PR](https://github.com/benoyj20/Computer-Vision-For-Object-History-Tracking-/pull/5)), and vendor tiling tools fuse inside an edge band ([DeGirum tiling guide](https://docs.degirum.com/hailo/intermediate-guides/tiling)). A regression test in `scripts/check-detector.mjs` should cover both directions: a truncated box in window A plus the full box in window B should count as one, while two adjacent same-class symbols in one window should still count as two. The same arithmetic exposes the window cap. An A0 sheet with a crop-trained model (pxPerPt 3, 416 px input, 32 px overlap) needs **27 × 19 = 513 windows**, one over the limit. The scan then silently falls back to the CV tier on exactly the sheets tiling was built for. Three changes address this. Skipping near-blank windows applies the content-gating idea of ROI-Gated SAHI with a trivial ink-coverage gate ([arXiv 2608.23923](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23923v1)). A time budget can replace the hard count. Filtering by class before the detection cap stops a dense lookalike class from starving the clicked one. Any cap that is hit should surface as a "count may be truncated" warning, because a takeoff count must never be silently capped. If fusion moves to a spatial index, SAHI 0.12.5's STRtree rewrite is the model. It cut a crowded 33,337-box case from **47 s to 135 ms** ([SAHI Discussion #1420](https://github.com/obss/sahi/discussions/1420)).

The 2024-2026 symbol-spotting literature changes the plan for the exemplar tier more than for the detector. Raster detectors on electrical and MEP sheets report **76-83% mAP@50** on small, mostly private datasets:

- SkeySpot: 82.5% on 45 scanned electrical plans ([arXiv 2508.10449](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10449v1)).
- Jamieson et al. (IJDAR 2024): 79-83% on plumbing and HVAC sheets ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10032-024-00492-9)).

None of them reports tile fusion or sheet-level count error, and their licences block reuse: SkeySpot is built on AGPL YOLOv8, and its DELP dataset is non-commercial. One finding does support Patch 5's premise. A graph-proposal plus self-supervised Siamese detector found **5 unseen MEP equipment types at F1 0.95** ([Journal of Building Engineering, 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352710225027639)). The most directly relevant experiment is **PlanSpot**, an unreviewed two-commit repo. It runs one-shot spotting on FloorPlanCAD over 369 episodes. Pixel normalised cross-correlation (NCC) beat frozen DINOv2-S on **small symbols (F1 0.767 vs 0.664)**, DINOv2 won on medium symbols, and recall sat near 0.4 for both ([GitHub planspot](https://github.com/dzakybd/planspot)). Read alongside the **DAVE** detect-then-verify counter (CVPR 2024, MIT licence) ([GitHub DAVE](https://github.com/jerpelhan/DAVE)), this suggests a hybrid Patch 5:

1. Generate high-recall NCC or shape candidates.
2. Verify them with DINOv2 similarity.
3. Cut with an adaptive largest-gap threshold.
4. Search 8 dihedral poses.
5. Run the same seam fusion as the detector.

Newer open-world counters add negative exemplars, a "not this" prompt, which suits lookalike confusion such as a power outlet (GPO) against a switch. Examples are **CountGD++** (CVPR 2026) ([arXiv 2512.23351](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.23351)) and **T-Rex-Omni** ([arXiv 2511.08997](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.08997)). Those models are GPU-class, but the idea transfers to a cheap Alt-click. GeCo and GeCo2 lead exemplar-only benchmarks but sit on SAM backbones, so they are distillation teachers, not browser candidates ([GitHub GeCo](https://github.com/jerpelhan/GeCo)). For Patch 1, **SynthPID** (CVPR 2026 Workshops, P&IDs) found that template-scattered synthetic data reaches only about 33% edge accuracy, while re-rendering perturbed real layouts reaches **63.8% with no real training drawings** ([CVPRW 2026](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/AI4RWC/papers/Prasad_SynthPID_PID_digitization_from_Topology-Preserving_Synthetic_Data_CVPRW_2026_paper.pdf)). For the generator, that makes layout realism (downlights on ceiling grids, outlets on walls) the next lever after degradation.

## Dynamic Fill misses rooms because escalation only reacts to leaks

The shipped Patch 2 explains both open fill bugs. `dynamicfill.ts` closes any short horizontal or vertical ink-free run that has ink at both ends. It escalates the closing radius through `GAP_HALF = [0, 8, 16, 28, 44]` only while the fill exceeds `MAX_FILL_FRACTION = 0.4` of the page ([dynamicfill.ts](../src/engine/dynamicfill.ts)). An interior door between two rooms in a sealed building never pushes the fill past 40%, so it is never bridged. Meanwhile the strip between a wall and a parallel bench line has ink at both ends, so it gets closed and area goes missing. The learned state of the art does not make this disappear. **Raster2Seq** (SIGGRAPH 2026) sets a new CubiCasa5K room F1 of **88.7**, but window and door F1 stays near **78%**, with RoomFormer marginally ahead at 78.5 ([DOI 10.1145/3799902.3811124](https://doi.org/10.1145/3799902.3811124)). A learned door gate would still miss about one opening in five. A 2026 preprint also finds that detection-style readouts (junction and opening heatmaps) beat sequence decoding on real, large, scan-like plans ([arXiv 2608.25608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25608)). That is the architecture easiest to shrink for ONNX Runtime Web. The licensing news matters most for the plan. **ResPlan** offers 17,000 plans with vector walls, doors, rooms and typed `via_door` room graphs under **CC BY 4.0** data and MIT code ([GitHub ResPlan](https://github.com/m-agour/ResPlan); [arXiv 2508.14006](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14006v1)). That removes the CC BY-NC blocker the patch plan flagged for Patch 2 Tier B, subject to a legal check of its real-estate-listing provenance.

The cheapest fixes come from older wall-detection work and robotics, not from the new models. De las Heras et al. show that walls are recognised by structure (parallel lines, longer than thick, in dominant directions), and that thickness alone fails across drawing notations ([ICDAR 2013](https://refbase.cvc.uab.cat/files/HFV2013.pdf); [IJDAR 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10032-013-0215-2)). That yields a **collinear-continuation gate** for furniture-line closing. Close a run only if the ink at both ends continues along the run's own axis for at least about 1.5 times the estimated wall thickness. A door gap in a wall passes, because the wall continues on both sides. A vertical run across a bench strip fails, because above and below it the ink is a single stroke. The gate fits the existing single-pass precompute, costs hours, and is the highest-value first move. No 2023-2026 peer-reviewed paper evaluates furniture-aware gap closing, so it is an engineering inference.

For door bridging, robotics map segmentation offers a mature toolbox. **Bormann et al.** (ICRA 2016) compared four open implementations and found:

- Morphological and distance-transform splitting are the cheapest.
- Voronoi splitting has the best recall with furniture present ([paper](https://mirror.umd.edu/roswiki/attachments/ipa_room_segmentation/article.pdf)).
- Third-party benchmarks confirm that all four degrade with clutter ([ROSE², arXiv 2203.03519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03519)).

The newer DUDE decomposition splits free space at doorway-like constrictions in about **2.5 ms per update** ([arXiv 2606.13727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13727)). Applied inside one fill, a Euclidean distance transform can find necks of half a door width whose two sides end at wall endpoints. Cutting there separates two rooms even when nothing leaks. On architectural plans, door swings are the highest-precision cue. A quarter-circle arc hinged on a wall end has a radius equal to the opening width ([Plancio symbols guide](https://plancio.com/en/floor-plans/floor-plan-symbols/)). Open-source detectors exist for both raster and vector arcs, though neither is peer-reviewed ([matchline PR #571](https://github.com/anchapin/matchline/pull/571); [matchline PR #791](https://github.com/anchapin/matchline/pull/791)).

pdf2takeoff also has an advantage most of this literature lacks: born-digital text. Ahmed et al. and de las Heras et al. already use text in room detection ([Ahmed, DAS 2012](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230675743_Automatic_Room_Detection_and_Room_Labeling_from_Architectural_Floor_Plans)). If a fill contains two or more room labels from the PDF text layer, it has merged rooms by definition. That replaces "never detected" with "detected whenever the plan labels its rooms". Area plausibility at the calibrated scale gives a second semantic escalation trigger.

The researchers' ranked order for these fixes is:

1. The collinear-continuation gate.
2. Label-count and area-plausibility triggers.
3. Door-width closing, always on once scale is calibrated.
4. EDT neck cuts, applied automatically only with corroboration (wall endpoints at both ends plus an arc, a door tag or labels on both sides), so L-shaped rooms are not cut at the elbow.
5. Door-arc chords.
6. Endpoint-pair bridging as the orientation-free long-term replacement for axis-aligned closing.

A small learned opening heatmap trained on ResPlan comes after all six.

## Text reading needs a drafting rule, not a bigger model

### A reading-direction rule fixes upside-down vertical dimensions

The vertical-dimension bug is a canonicalisation error, not a recognition one. `ocrCorridor` swaps endpoints so that a.x ≤ b.x, then rotates by atan2. For a near-vertical segment, dx is a fraction of a pixel, so the angle lands at roughly +90° or −90° depending on click order and sub-pixel jitter. The crop therefore comes out upside-down about half the time ([ocr.ts](../src/app/ocr.ts)). Every strong 2025-2026 dimension reader normalises orientation before recognition rather than trusting the recognizer. Khan et al. crop oriented annotation boxes with a rotation-aware detector ([arXiv 2506.17374](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.17374)). **eDOCr2** (MDPI Machines 2025, peer-reviewed) reports under 1% character error rate ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1702/13/3/254)). Its source code snaps each box angle to 5° steps and folds it into one canonical reading half-plane, rather than using an orientation classifier ([ocr_pipelines.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/javvi51/edocr2/main/edocr2/tools/ocr_pipelines.py)). The drafting convention supplies the rule: aligned dimensions read from the bottom, or **from the right for vertical dimensions** ([CCCOnline Blueprint Reading](https://pressbooks.ccconline.org/bluepringcad/wp-json/pressbooks/v2/chapters/126); [NPTEL](https://archive.nptel.ac.in/content/storage2/courses/112103019/module1/lec3/3.html)).

The fix needs no model. Fold the segment angle into [−90°, 90°), then map anything within about 10° of +90° to −90°. Rhino applies the same 10° tolerance ([McNeel forum](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/dimension-is-upside-down/127618)). For sheets that break the convention, the researchers propose reading both 0° and 180° within ±20° of vertical. The winner is chosen by four tests, in order:

1. Does it parse?
2. Does it avoid a leading zero? An upside-down "1200" reads as "0021".
3. What is the word confidence?
4. Does it agree with the scale consensus?

Confidence alone cannot separate 6 from 9. A digit whitelist on the corridor worker follows eDOCr2's digits-only acceptance of single glyphs. Tesseract.js supports one, while its orientation detection needs the legacy engine and works at page level ([tesseract.js API](https://github.com/naptha/tesseract.js/blob/master/docs/api.md)).

The learned upgrade path for Patch 3 is now much shorter. **`@paddleocr/paddleocr-js`** is an official **Apache-2.0** browser SDK (npm v0.4.2, published April 2026). It runs on ONNX Runtime Web with PP-OCRv5 and PP-OCRv6 small and tiny models, and offers an optional `use_textline_orientation` stage ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@paddleocr/paddleocr-js)). It replaces the hand-built ONNX port the plan assumed. Its text-line orientation model's **99.42%** accuracy is vendor-reported and measured on general text lines ([PaddleOCR update notes](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/@PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR/blob/da92b8ce0404e07646dfb449ad90af9663601e22/docs/update/update.en.md)). A 2025 preprint finds that adding a rotation classifier improves OCR on rotated input by up to 14% for closed models and up to 4× for open ones. That work measures whole pages, not drawing crops ([arXiv 2511.04161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04161v1)). For any later learned reader, a small fine-tuned model clearly beats a prompted frontier one on drawings. Zero-shot GPT-4o managed only **35.51% F1** on GD&T extraction, against large relative gains for a fine-tuned 0.23B Florence-2 ([arXiv 2411.03707](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.03707)). No source reports 180° confusion rates on short numeric strings, so the extra gain from dual reading over canonicalisation alone is unknown until measured.

The `read_text` bug has a source-level fix. In pdf.js, a text item's `transform` is the full text-rendering matrix, and `width` is the advance *along the baseline* ([pdf.js evaluator.js](https://github.com/mozilla/pdf.js/blob/master/src/core/evaluator.js)). `read_text` ignores the rotation terms and offsets split words along +x ([mcp.ts](../src/headless/mcp.ts)), so words in rotated or vertical items get wrong positions and boxes. The fix is to derive unit vectors from the matrix's first four terms, place each word centre along them, and report an angle. A regression check should use a rotated "1:100" and a vertical "3600". Given AEC-Bench's evidence that agents lean on text extraction, this fix is cheap and has unusually high leverage.

### Scale should be a vote among sources, not the first match

Academic work on inferring drawing scale is sparse. The strongest consensus method found is a tool, not a paper. **PlanMetric** (MIT licence, not peer-reviewed) works in three steps:

1. It assigns OCR values to dimension spans.
2. It runs RANSAC-style scalar hypotheses per axis.
3. It returns an explicit "conflict" or "incomplete" result instead of inventing a scale.

Its README is candid that "internally consistent wrong dimensions still produce a wrong accepted scale" ([GitHub planmetric](https://github.com/crown-sports/planmetric)). Two peer-reviewed papers support consensus. Lv et al. (CVPR 2021) recover scale by clustering dimension evidence ([CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Lv_Residential_Floor_Plan_Recognition_and_Reconstruction_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf)). A 2025 Buildings paper reports **over 95% scale-calculation accuracy** from detected dimension endpoints plus OCR on 5,000 plans ([doi:10.3390/buildings15071178](https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings15071178)). Applied to pdf2takeoff, each calibrated dimension gives one scale observation. A 1-D RANSAC with a band of about ±2% across those observations, the title-block ratio, any imperial note and any graphic bar then flags misreads by their factor:

- ×6 is the colon bug: "1200" read as 1:200.
- ×10 suggests a unit slip.
- A clean √2 or 2 suggests a sheet printed at reduced size.

Viewport partitions (already shipped) must split the votes, because consistent wrong evidence still passes.

Within that framework, the three parser gaps get targeted grammars. Today the ratio parser deliberately requires a colon, because a bare "1200" is more often a dimension ([engine/ocr.ts](../src/engine/ocr.ts)). The proposed relaxation has three parts. Accept colon substitutes (semicolon, period, pipe, i, l) or a space only within one token of a SCALE, @ or AT keyword. Accept a bare "1NNN" only directly after SCALE, only when NNN is in the preferred series, and only as a low-confidence vote that needs consensus before it is applied. Abstain on "NTS".

The preferred-series prior comes from ISO 5455's 1, 2, 5 × 10ⁿ reductions, plus the common AEC 25 and 250 ([ISO 5455 adoption](https://descargas.epconsgtmo.co.cu/Normalizacion%20Actualizadas/Normas%20de%20la%20construccion/Normas%20De%20Dibujo/NC%20ISO%205455-2004%20Dibujo%20T%C3%A9cnico.Escalas.pdf)). Imperial scales arrive as human strings: Archicad exports "1/4" = 1'-0"" rather than 1:48 ([Graphisoft community](https://community.graphisoft.com/t5/Design-forum/Drawing-Scale-to-TITLE-OBJECT/td-p/225046)). A normalised architectural grammar therefore computes the ratio as 12 divided by the fraction: 1/4 gives 48, 3/16 gives 64, 1/8 gives 96. An engineering grammar maps 1" = N' to 12N, so 20' gives 240. Both should snap to the standard imperial table ([LibreCAD scales](https://docs.librecad.org/en/latest/appx/scales.html)).

No detector for architectural graphic scale bars was found. Two adjacent fields show where the difficulty lies. In SEM microscopy, bar *detection* reaches 99.2% mAP50 but label OCR only **75% F1** ([arXiv 2510.11260](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11260)). Chart parsing offers a transferable recipe: Hungarian-match tick labels to ticks, then fit pixel-to-value with RANSAC ([arXiv 2608.06062](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06062)). A deterministic bar tier would follow the same pattern. It would look for a line with three or more ticks or alternating blocks near a SCALE keyword, with labels in arithmetic progression, and require three inliers. Its result counts as a vote, never an override. Server-side, the 2026 EC3 papers make **scale** an explicit field in schema-constrained title-block extraction, with Qwen3-VL at **0.911 F1** overall ([arXiv 2607.18997](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.18997); [EC3 2026 #252](https://ec-3.org/publication/ec32026_252/)). Even then, the extracted ratio should feed the same consensus rather than bypass it.

## The unused vector layer outranks any new model on CAD PDFs

The single largest opportunity across all five threads is one the code review did not name. pdf2takeoff depends on pdf.js 5.x but never calls `getOperatorList`, so every count and fill runs on rasters. Vector-native symbol spotting has advanced fast:

| Method | PQ with CAD layer prior | PQ without prior | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| SymPoint (35M parameters) | 83.3 | (not reported) | [arXiv 2401.10556](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10556) |
| SymPoint-V2 | 90.1 | 83.2 | [arXiv 2505.23395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23395) |
| DPSS | (not reported) | 86.2 (secondary source) | [alphaxiv overview](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2503.22346v1) |
| VecFormer (NeurIPS 2025) | 91.1 | 88.4 | [arXiv 2505.23395](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23395) |

The no-prior column is the one that applies to PDFs, which usually lose CAD layers. All four are GPU models trained on research-only data, so they belong on an optional server at most. The deterministic alternative is older and better suited to the browser. Nayef and Breuel built a symbol library by clustering repeated patterns, reporting **98.8% recall and 97.31% precision** on grouping ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-36824-0_7)). Graph-path hashing spots symbols robustly in line drawings ([Pattern Recognition 2013](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0031320312004359)). The October 2026 TempFit preprint re-frames learned spotting as retrieving a template and fitting its pose ([arXiv 2610.17776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17776)).

A vector tier would partly sidestep three of the open problems:

- **Counting:** a click-one, find-all match on hashed path signatures needs no tiles, so it has no seams and no window cap.
- **Room fill:** stroke widths from `setLineWidth` separate heavy wall lines from thin furniture lines exactly, and arc-plus-leaf door detection yields exact chords for closing rooms.
- **Scale:** text-layer scale strings are exact, so the colon problem is OCR-only.

The cost is that building graphs from vector plans is slower than rasterising ([Carrara, EG-ICE 2025](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1781828/sdbdwmsyswh5qjjiqmby19z8r.eg-ice2025_Carrara.pdf)). It is also unverified whether common exporters emit blocks as Form XObjects, keep layers as optional content groups, or pre-split dashes. A check on the repo's sample PDFs should come before any build. This would be a new patch beyond the plan's six, sitting ahead of Patch 5 for born-digital sheets and behind it for scans.

## Conclusion

The research frontier has converged on the architecture pdf2takeoff already has: learned components propose, and deterministic components verify. It shows up as DAVE's detect-then-verify, Drawing-Checker's view matching before the VLM, SALI-FP's evidence gates and Handoff's orchestrated tools. What the papers almost never study is the deterministic glue in between: seam fusion, gap-closing rules, reading-direction folding and scale parsing. Every bug in today's review lives in that glue. Better models will not fix it. pdf2takeoff's own regression cases will, with seam-straddling symbols, rooms joined by doors, bench strips, vertical dimensions in both click orders, and colon-less, imperial and graphic scales. Those cases are the evidence that matters, and the literature can only suggest which fixes to try first.

The same gap is also an opening. No public benchmark measures length or area takeoff from a scaled drawing, by VLMs or by tools. The repo's synthetic generator already knows ground-truth scale, wall lengths and room areas. A small benchmark built from it would test VLM-only against agent-plus-tools. Combined with runs on AECV-Bench's counting set and an "H plus pdf2takeoff MCP" variant of AEC-Bench, it would turn the deterministic-numbers rule from a well-supported design choice into a measured result that pdf2takeoff could publish.
