# Reading text on engineering/construction drawings: dimensions, scales, title blocks, rotated text (gap-focused, 2023–2026)

Scope note: this extends `docs/research/ai-literature-review.md` §3 and `docs/research/patch-plan.md` Patch 3. Already covered there and **not re-reported**: Schönfelder et al. 2024 (AutoCon), Ying et al. 2026 (Frontiers), Scheibel et al. 2021, Schlagenhauf 2022, Fletcher–Kasturi 1988, PP-OCR 2020, TrOCR, GOT-OCR2, DeepSeek-OCR/dots.ocr/MinerU/olmOCR/Docling/Donut, Lombardi et al. 2025 (title blocks), Shen et al. 2022. Method caveat: arXiv, MDPI, Hugging Face and paddleocr.ai were blocked by the sandbox proxy, so most paper figures below come from abstracts/search snippets, not full texts. ## 1. Dimension recognition in engineering drawings (mechanical + AEC), 2023–2026

### Takeaway
Mechanical-drawing work (eDOCr → eDOCr2 → the NTU Singapore VLM line) is now the most mature dimension-reading literature. The 2025–2026 consensus pipeline is to detect oriented annotation boxes (YOLO-OBB or DBNet), normalise them to horizontal, and recognise them with a digit-restricted recognizer or a small fine-tuned VLM (Florence-2 or Donut). Zero-shot frontier VLMs remain poor at this. AEC-specific dimension-to-line association is still thinly published.

### Cited Findings
- **eDOCr** — Villena Toro, Wiberg, Tarkian (Linköping), *Optical character recognition on engineering drawings to achieve automation in production quality control*, Frontiers in Manufacturing Technology, 2023 (peer-reviewed). Splits drawings into info blocks/tables, feature control frames and the rest, then runs a separate OCR pipeline on each (keras-ocr based). Reports 90% detection precision/recall, 94% recognition F1 and 8% CER. These are overall text figures, not dimension-only. — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmtec.2023.1154132/pdf); [PyPI eDOCr](https://pypi.org/project/eDOCr/)
- **eDOCr2** — Villena Toro et al., MDPI *Machines* 13(3):254, 2025 (peer-reviewed; SSRN preprint 10.2139/ssrn.5045921). Segments drawings into information blocks, dimensions and feature control frames. Reports 93.75% text recall and <1% CER on a mixed-source benchmark. Adds Qwen2-VL-7B / GPT-4o *after* segmentation only to verify, filter or retrieve, not as the primary reader. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-1702/13/3/254); [GitHub javvi51/edocr2](https://github.com/javvi51/edocr2)
- eDOCr2 rotation handling (read from source code). The detector returns rotated boxes. `postprocess_detection` computes each box's angle and snaps it to 5° steps. `adjust_angle` maps angles near +90° to `round(α/5)*5 − 180`, so it folds every box into one canonical reading half-plane instead of using a learned orientation classifier. Single-glyph boxes are rotated 90° CCW and accepted only if the recognizer output `isdigit()`. Tolerance stacks are split before recognition (`check_tolerances`). — [ocr_pipelines.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/javvi51/edocr2/main/edocr2/tools/ocr_pipelines.py)
- **Florence-2 fine-tuned for GD&T** — *Fine-Tuning Vision-Language Model for Automated Engineering Drawing Information Extraction*, arXiv 2411.03707, 2024 (preprint; author list not confirmed from snippets, appears to be the NTU group below). Uses 400 expert-annotated drawings and full fine-tuning of 0.23B Florence-2. Zero-shot baselines: GPT-4o P 59.03% / R 25.39% / F1 35.51%; Claude-3.5-Sonnet R 37.27% with a 55.99% hallucination rate. Fine-tuned Florence-2 improves F1 by 52.40% *relative* to the best baseline and cuts hallucination by 43.15% relative. — [arXiv 2411.03707](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.03707)
- **From Drawings to Decisions** — Khan, Chen, Yong, Tan, Feng, Moon, arXiv 2506.17374, 2025 (preprint). Uses **YOLOv11-obb** (rotation-aware) to crop annotations as oriented patches, then fine-tuned Donut / Florence-2 parse them into structured fields. 1,367 mechanical drawings, 9 categories (GD&T, measures, material, text). It states plainly that generic OCR fails on "complex layouts, engineering symbols, and rotated text". — [arXiv 2506.17374](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.17374)
- **Multi-stage hybrid framework for multi-view drawings** — same group, arXiv 2510.21862, 2025 (preprint). Uses YOLOv11 layout detection plus two OCR-free Donut parsers. The *numerical* parser reaches F1 0.963, but the *alphabetical* (notes/title-block text) parser only 0.672. Numbers are the easy part once cropped. — [arXiv 2510.21862](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21862)
- AEC: *A Hybrid Deep Learning and Rule-Based Method for Architectural Drawing Vectorization and CAD Reconstruction*, Buildings 16(5):1043, 2026 (peer-reviewed). Uses a YOLO detector for grid markers and dimension annotations, then OCR inside those regions for pixel-to-metric conversion. The abstract does not describe explicit text-to-dimension-line binding. — [doi:10.3390/buildings16051043](https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings16051043)
- AEC: Chang, Lv, Wang, Pang, Liu, *Raster Image-Based House-Type Recognition and 3D Reconstruction*, Buildings 15(7):1178, 2025 (peer-reviewed). Uses YOLOv8 + Shi–Tomasi corners to find dimension endpoints and OFA-OCR to read the value. Reports **scale calculation accuracy >95%** and element P/R >87/88% on a 5,000-plan dataset. — [doi:10.3390/buildings15071178](https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings15071178)
- Adjacent 2026 AEC work found but not read in full: knowledge-graph validation of 2D bridge drawings (AutoCon 2026, [ScienceDirect S0926580526004541](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0926580526004541)) and *Learning to Ground Before Reading* (compact VLMs for PCB drawings, [arXiv 2608.29268](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.29268)). Both follow the same pattern: localise first, then read.

### Inferences
- Every strong 2025–26 system **normalises orientation before recognition** (OBB detector + canonicalisation). None relies on the recognizer to cope with rotation. pdf2takeoff's corridor crop already does the normalisation, so its open bug is in the canonicalisation rule (see §3), not in the approach.
- eDOCr2 accepts single-glyph reads only if they are pure digits. A cheap equivalent for us is to run the corridor recognizer with a numeric whitelist (`tessedit_char_whitelist`, see §3) and treat non-numeric reads as rejects.
- Fine-tuned 0.2B VLMs beat zero-shot frontier models by large margins on drawing fields. If a learned reader is ever added, a small fine-tuned model beats a big prompted one. This is consistent with the existing "numbers come from deterministic tools" rule.

### Gaps
- I found no 2023–2026 peer-reviewed AEC paper that reports an explicit **dimension-line ↔ value association** accuracy (Scheibel 2021, already covered, is still the reference). The term search in Automation in Construction / JCCE returned nothing specific.
- No public AEC dimension benchmark with rotated/vertical labels was found. eDOCr2's test set is mechanical.

## 2. Automatic scale detection: title blocks, scale bars, dimension consensus

### Takeaway
Academic work on drawing-scale inference is sparse. The best "consensus" method found is an MIT-licensed open-source tool (PlanMetric), not a paper. It assigns OCR'd values to spans, then runs RANSAC over scalar scale hypotheses with per-dimension residuals and explicit "conflict" outcomes. Scale bars have recent pipelines in microscopy and historical maps (detect → OCR labels → fit), plus a directly transferable tick-to-value calibration recipe from chart parsing.

### Cited Findings
- **PlanMetric** (crown-sports, GitHub, MIT, v0.1.0; tool, *not peer-reviewed*). Method: one-to-one geometric assignment of OCR text to dimension spans, then "deterministic RANSAC-style scalar hypotheses", then a supported fit per axis, with separate x/y scales and a stretch diagnostic. Its demo uses 8 supporting pairs, rejects 2 inconsistent ones and recovers 10 mm/px. It returns an explicit incomplete/conflict result "without inventing a scale". The README admits the limit: "**Internally consistent wrong dimensions still produce a wrong accepted scale**". Its evaluation is synthetic and makes no real-image OCR claim. — [GitHub planmetric](https://github.com/crown-sports/planmetric)
- Lv et al., *Residential Floor Plan Recognition and Reconstruction*, CVPR 2021 (peer-reviewed). Recovers scale by detecting line segments (pixel lengths), keypoints and **cluster analysis** across dimension evidence, an early consensus approach. — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Lv_Residential_Floor_Plan_Recognition_and_Reconstruction_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf)
- A patch-based CNN floor-plan study uses dimension lines when present and otherwise falls back to the pixel width of the thinnest wall (heuristic prior). — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/356957045_Automatic_Extraction_of_Indoor_Spatial_Information_from_Floor_Plan_Image_A_Patch-Based_Deep_Learning_Methodology_Application_on_Large-Scale_Complex_Buildings)
- **Scale bars, microscopy**: Chen, Yang, Zhang, Ahmed, Wang, *LLM-Assisted Automated Scale Bar Detection and Extraction for SEM Images*, arXiv 2510.11260, 2025 (preprint). Bar detection reaches P 100% / R 95.8% / mAP50 99.2%, but **label OCR only P 89% / R 65% / F1 75%**. An LLM agent verifies the result. Reading the label, not finding the bar, is the bottleneck. — [arXiv 2510.11260](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11260)
- **Scale bars, historical maps**: Donaldson, CaGIS 2026 poster abstract. A multi-stage YOLO-style detection + VLM interpretation pipeline on Library of Congress maps, evaluated against a manual set (abstract only, no numbers seen). — [CaGIS 2026](https://cartogis.org/docs/conferences/CaGIS_2026/abstracts/poster/Donaldson_poster_abstract_CaGIS_2026.pdf)
- Classical scale bar recipe (MDPI *Materials*, Dec 2025, doi 10.3390/ma18245579; peer-reviewed, seen via table page only): find the black bar, measure pixel length with a **Hough transform**, read the physical length with OCR, and **ask the user if OCR fails**. — [PMC table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12734435/table/array1/)
- Tick-label calibration from chart parsing (2026 bar-chart extraction work, e.g. Bar-JEPA arXiv 2608.06062; attribution from search summary). Detect ticks, OCR labels with PaddleOCR, **Hungarian-match labels to ticks** with a distance gate, then **RANSAC line fit pixel→value**. This maps one-to-one onto a graphic scale bar (0 / 1 / 2 / 5 m labels). — [arXiv 2608.06062](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06062)
- Practice (vendor/blog, *not peer-reviewed*): Exayard advises comparing a known dimension's pixel length with the title block and scale bar, and investigating conflicts rather than taking the first value ([Exayard](https://exayard.com/blog/floor-plan-scales)). FloorScan claims a source priority of vector dimensions → title-block scale → standard door widths, with a confidence score ([FloorScan](https://floorscan.ai/en/features/floor-plan-measurement)). MONA refuses pixel-only size inference because of silent scale errors ([MONA](https://monaverse.com/help/floor-plan-dimensions-and-scale)).
- Preferred-scale priors: ISO 5455 reduction series is 1:2, 1:5, 1:10, 1:20, 1:50, 1:100, 1:200, 1:500, 1:1000 … 1:10 000. Other scales are allowed when derived as integer × power of 10, or in exceptional cases. — [NC-ISO 5455 (national adoption)](https://descargas.epconsgtmo.co.cu/Normalizacion%20Actualizadas/Normas%20de%20la%20construccion/Normas%20De%20Dibujo/NC%20ISO%205455-2004%20Dibujo%20T%C3%A9cnico.Escalas.pdf); [IS 10713](https://law.resource.org/pub/in/bis/S01/is.10713.1983.pdf)
- Imperial priors: 1/8"=1'-0" = 1:96, 3/16" = 1:64, 1/4" = 1:48, 3/32" = 1:128. Engineer's 1"=20' = 1:240. — [LibreCAD scales table](https://docs.librecad.org/en/latest/appx/scales.html); [Wikipedia: Scale ruler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scale_ruler)
- Archicad stores the scale as a formatted string "1/4" = 1'-0"" while treating it internally as 1:48. Exported text is the human string, not a ratio. — [Graphisoft community](https://community.graphisoft.com/t5/Design-forum/Drawing-Scale-to-TITLE-OBJECT/td-p/225046)

### Inferences
- **Dimension consensus is the strongest verifier available to pdf2takeoff.** Each (clicked segment, read value) pair is one scale observation, `s_i = value_i / length_pt_i`. With ≥3 observations, a 1-D RANSAC/median with a ~2% inlier band identifies misreads. These include 6↔9 flips, upside-down "1200"→"0021", and a colon-less "1200" read as 1:200, which would differ from consensus by 6× or 10×. With one observation the title-block ratio is the second vote. PlanMetric's caveat applies: consensus cannot catch consistent wrong evidence (e.g. a whole viewport at a different scale), so keep per-viewport calibration (already shipped).
- Snapping to the preferred-scale series is a cheap prior: accept a ratio outright only if N is in ISO 5455 (×1, ×2.5, ×5 variants such as 1:25, 1:250, 1:1250 are common in AEC) or the imperial table. Otherwise mark it low-confidence and require consensus.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed paper was found that formalises scale *verification* from multiple dimensions on construction drawings. PlanMetric's docs/methods.md references were not retrieved.
- No published detector specifically for **architectural graphic scale bars** was found (alternating-block bars, "0 1 2 5 10 m"). Microscopy and maps are the nearest analogues.
- No source quantified how often PDF sheets are printed at a non-nominal size (A1 → A3), which silently invalidates a title-block "1:100 @ A1".

## 3. Rotated/vertical text OCR: orientation, engines, what runs in a browser

### Takeaway
Treat orientation in two steps. A deterministic canonicalisation from the known segment angle, using the drafting convention that aligned dimensions read from the bottom or the right, removes the 0/180 ambiguity for nearly all vertical dimensions. A tiny 0/180 text-line classifier or a two-way read is the fallback. Browser-ready options now include an official Apache-2.0 PaddleOCR.js SDK (PP-OCRv5/v6 + optional textline-orientation stage). Tesseract.js OSD needs the legacy engine and is page-oriented.

### Cited Findings
- **Root cause in pdf2takeoff (read from code).** `ocrCorridor` swaps endpoints so that `ra.x ≤ rb.x`, then rotates by `θ = atan2(dy, dx)`. For a near-vertical segment, dx is ±a fraction of a pixel, so θ lands at **either ≈ +90° or ≈ −90° depending on click order and sub-pixel jitter**. The crop is therefore rotated upright or upside-down about half the time, which matches the reported symptom. — [src/app/ocr.ts](/home/user/pdf2takeoff/src/app/ocr.ts) (lines ~151–175)
- Drafting convention: in the **aligned** system, dimension text reads from the bottom for horizontal and **from the right side for vertical dimensions**. The unidirectional system reads everything from the bottom. — [CCCOnline Blueprint Reading/CAD (pressbooks)](https://pressbooks.ccconline.org/bluepringcad/wp-json/pressbooks/v2/chapters/126); [NPTEL Engineering Drawing](https://archive.nptel.ac.in/content/storage2/courses/112103019/module1/lec3/3.html). ISO 129-1:2004 §5.6.2 defines the angle limits (cited in a LibreOffice bug, not verified from the standard) — [LibreOffice bug 149883](https://www.libreoffice.org/bugzilla/show_bug.cgi?id=149883). Rhino treats dimensions up to 10° left of vertical as following the right-side convention (software behaviour, not ISO). — [McNeel forum](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/dimension-is-upside-down/127618)
- **PaddleOCR text-line orientation (0°/180°)**: release notes report `PP-LCNet_x0_25_textline_ori` at 98.85% accuracy after a +3.3 pt update. A new `PP-LCNet_x1_0_textline_ori` at 99.42% became the default for the OCR / PP-StructureV3 pipelines (vendor-reported). A 0.96 MB size appears on a Hugging Face card whose label may be mismatched (unverified). — [PaddleOCR update notes](https://glama.ai/mcp/servers/@PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR/blob/da92b8ce0404e07646dfb449ad90af9663601e22/docs/update/update.en.md); [HF PP-LCNet_x1_0_textline_ori](https://huggingface.co/PaddlePaddle/PP-LCNet_x1_0_textline_ori); [PaddleOCR 3.0 Technical Report, arXiv 2507.05595](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05595). The separate 4-class document orientation model (PP-LCNet_x1_0_doc_ori) is page-level. — [PaddleOCR docs](https://www.paddleocr.ai/v3.0.0/en/version3.x/module_usage/doc_img_orientation_classification.html)
- **PaddleOCR.js** (`@paddleocr/paddleocr-js`, npm v0.4.2, **Apache-2.0**, first published 2026-04-02, from the PaddlePaddle/PaddleOCR repo; checked by unpacking the package). Browser SDK on ONNX Runtime Web + OpenCV.js. Built-in PP-OCRv5 (`PP-OCRv5_mobile_det/rec`) and **PP-OCRv6_small / PP-OCRv6_tiny** model pairs, worker mode, and WebGPU/threaded WASM (requires COOP/COEP headers). The dist contains a `use_textline_orientation` option that inserts a TextLineOrientation stage (default off). Unpacked package ≈ 23.8 MB (models fetched separately). — [npm @paddleocr/paddleocr-js](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@paddleocr/paddleocr-js); [PaddleOCR browser deployment docs](https://www.paddleocr.ai/latest/en/version3.x/inference_deployment/cross_platform/browser.html)
- Community alternatives (self-reported, *not peer-reviewed*): `ppu-paddle-ocr` (PP-OCRv5 mobile via onnxruntime-web; author claims 99.22% char accuracy on receipts) and `ffocr` (PaddleOCR ONNX with webgpu/webnn/webgl/wasm EPs). — [Medium post](https://medium.com/@awalariansyah7/deterministic-ocr-in-javascript-paddleocr-for-node-bun-deno-and-the-browser-4c5c3c3e7512); [jsr ppu-paddle-ocr](https://jsr.io/@snowfluke/ppu-paddle-ocr)
- **Tesseract.js**: `worker.detect()` runs OSD but **requires `legacyCore: true, legacyLang: true`** (not enabled by default). `setParameters({tessedit_char_whitelist: '0123456789'})` is supported. — [tesseract.js docs/api.md](https://github.com/naptha/tesseract.js/blob/master/docs/api.md). Tesseract OSD returns rotation + script with a confidence. psm 0 = OSD only, psm 12 = sparse text with OSD. — [Tesseract man page](https://man.archlinux.org/man/tesseract.1)
- **docTR** (Mindee, Apache-2.0): exposes `crop_orientation_predictor` (per-word) and `page_orientation_predictor`, plus `assume_straight_pages=False` for rotated boxes and `straighten_pages`. Each word in the output carries a `crop_orientation` field. — [docTR README](https://github.com/mindee/doctr); [docTR using_models](https://github.com/mindee/doctr/blob/main/docs/source/using_doctr/using_models.rst)
- **Seeing Straight: Document Orientation Detection for Efficient OCR** — Goswami, Ravi, Kolla, Faraz, Khan, Akash, Khatri, Agarwal (Krutrim AI / OLA Electric), arXiv 2511.04161, Nov 2025, v2 Mar 2026 (preprint). Introduces the OCR-Rotation-Bench (ORB). Finds that general OCR engines *and* VLMs degrade badly on rotated input. Adding a rotation classifier gives up to +14% for closed models and up to 4× for open-weights models. 4-class accuracy 96.81% on ORB-En (page-level documents, not drawing crops). — [arXiv 2511.04161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04161v1)
- `docorient` (PyPI, self-reported): a numpy horizontal/vertical test plus an ~8.5 MB MobileNetV2 ONNX 180° classifier. Claims 96.8% flip accuracy and 98% overall. — [PyPI docorient](https://pypi.org/project/docorient/)
- Small VLMs in browser: Transformers.js v3 demonstrates **Florence-2-base-ft on WebGPU** (fp16 vision encoder, q4 encoder/decoder). It warns that encoder-decoder models like Florence-2 are "extremely sensitive to quantization", especially the encoder. The demo task is captioning, not OCR. — [HF blog Transformers.js v3](https://www.huggingface.co/blog/transformersjs-v3)

### Inferences
- **Fix for the upside-down bug (no model needed):** canonicalise θ into the aligned-dimension reading half-plane instead of sorting by x. In raster y-down coordinates, upward-reading text has θ = −90°. So: compute θ from a→b. Fold it into [−90°, 90°) by adding/subtracting 180°. Then, if θ ≥ 90° − τ (τ ≈ 5–10°, Rhino uses 10°), subtract 180°. Vertical segments then always map to −90° (text read from the right), whatever the click order or jitter.
- **Fallback for non-conforming sheets** (unidirectional text, CAD exports that flip text, hand-drafted plans). Within ±20° of vertical, OCR both the canonical crop and its 180° rotation and pick the winner by: (1) parse success against `NUM_RE`; (2) no leading zero (upside-down "1200" reads as "0021", "600" as "009"); (3) Tesseract word confidence; (4) agreement with consensus scale (§2). 6↔9 and 0021-style flips cannot be resolved by confidence alone, so (2) and (4) carry the weight. Cost is one extra small-strip OCR call only near vertical.
- A unidirectional sheet puts vertical-dimension text *horizontal*. The corridor already includes ±20 pt bands, but a θ-rotated crop turns that text vertical. So when the rotated read fails, also try an unrotated crop around the segment midpoint.
- Learned upgrade path: PaddleOCR.js with `use_textline_orientation` is now an official, Apache-2.0, browser-native route for patch-plan Patch 3. It replaces the planned hand-built ONNX port. Tesseract OSD is the wrong tool for short numeric strips: it is page-oriented and needs the heavier legacy data.

### Gaps
- No published accuracy for PP-LCNet textline orientation, docTR crop orientation or Tesseract OSD on **short numeric strings** (3–5 digits), where 180° ambiguity is intrinsic (e.g. "69", "96", "8008"). The reported 98–99% figures are on general text lines.
- No head-to-head benchmark of PaddleOCR v5/v6, docTR, Tesseract and Florence-2 on drawing dimension crops was found (Ying 2026, already covered, compares TrOCR/PARSeq/eDOCr only).
- PP-OCRv6 small/tiny accuracy and model sizes were not retrievable (paddleocr.ai blocked).

## 4. Title block / metadata extraction with VLMs and layout models (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
The 2026 papers (EC3 2026, Buro Happold / Birmingham City University) confirm a two-stage design. A detector (RF-DETR best mAP) or grounding VLM localises the title block. A schema-constrained VLM extracts fields, with **scale** as an explicit target field. Text fields are noticeably harder than numeric ones, and general-document-pretrained layout models transfer poorly to AEC sheets.

### Cited Findings
- **Benchmarking Deep Learning Approaches for AEC Engineering Drawing Layout Detection and Information Extraction** — Huang, Lombardi et al. (Birmingham City University / Buro Happold), EC3 2026 (peer-reviewed conference) / arXiv 2607.18997. Custom AEC layout dataset; compares 5 architectures. **RF-DETR mAP50 0.949**; **Qwen3-VL F1 0.911**. Models pre-trained on general document datasets suffer "domain interference". Treats the sheet as three layers: layout, title block, drawing content. — [arXiv 2607.18997](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.18997); [EC3 2026 #305](https://ec-3.org/publication/ec32026_305/)
- **Open-Source VLMs for Engineering Drawing Metadata: QWEN-VL Title-Block Detection and Information Extraction** — Peng et al., EC3 2026, Corfu (peer-reviewed conference). Qwen2.5-VL does visual grounding of the title block on full sheets, then **schema-constrained extraction** from the crop. Target fields: **scale**, drawing name, route. Argues open VLMs can replace multi-stage pipelines. — [EC3 2026 #252](https://ec-3.org/publication/ec32026_252/)
- Multi-view framework (arXiv 2510.21862, above): Donut parser on title-block/notes text reaches only F1 0.672, against 0.963 on numeric content. — [arXiv 2510.21862](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21862)
- Lombardi et al. 2025 (already in the repo review) is the baseline the 2026 papers build on. — [arXiv 2504.08645](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.08645)
- 2D bridge drawings: TU Dresden comparative study of text recognition tools plus lightweight post-recognition structured extraction (title not read in full). — [TU Dresden FIS](https://fis.tu-dresden.de/portal/de/publications/optimizing-2d-bridge-engineering-drawing-digitization-a-comparative-study-of-text-recognition-tools-and-development-of-lightweight-postrecognition-structured-information-extraction-methods(6b582fb3-657b-4eab-9a40-dd7486a4689c).html)

### Inferences
- For pdf2takeoff, in the browser: born-digital sheets carry the title-block scale in the PDF text layer, which is exact and needs no VLM. The cheap localisation prior is "bottom-right / right-edge strip, near a SCALE keyword", already partly implemented via keyword proximity in `findScaleRatio`. Qwen-VL-class extraction belongs on the MCP/server side, with the extracted ratio still validated by dimension consensus (§2), as the repo's VLM non-goal requires.
- **Text-layer bug affecting this (read from code and pdf.js source).** In pdf.js, a text item's `transform` is the full text-rendering matrix. For non-vertical fonts, `width` is the advance length *along the baseline* and `height = hypot(trm[2], trm[3])` ([pdf.js evaluator.js](https://github.com/mozilla/pdf.js/blob/master/src/core/evaluator.js), `ensureTextContentItem`/`runBidiTransform`). `read_text` in [src/headless/mcp.ts](/home/user/pdf2takeoff/src/headless/mcp.ts) (lines ~201–215) ignores `t[0..3]`. It offsets split words along +x and the height along +y, so words of rotated/vertical items get the wrong positions and bounding boxes. Fix: `u = (t0,t1)/|…|`, `v = (t2,t3)/|…|`; word centre = `(t4,t5) + u·(offset + pw/2) + v·(h/2)`; report `angle = atan2(t1,t0)`. Report the axis-aligned bounding box of the rotated rectangle as wPt/hPt, or add an angle field. CAD exports almost always use a rotated *horizontal* font (`font.vertical` false). True vertical fonts (CJK) take the other branch.

### Gaps
- Neither EC3 2026 paper's per-field accuracy for **scale** specifically could be retrieved (full texts blocked).
- No 2024–26 paper handles imperial scale strings or "NTS"/multiple-viewport scales in title blocks explicitly.

## 5. Recommendations mapped to the open problems

### Takeaway
All four open problems can be fixed mostly deterministically. Canonicalise the reading direction using the drafting convention. Add a dual-read fallback. Parse ratios with tolerant grammars snapped to standard-scale priors. Treat every scale source (title block, imperial note, graphic bar, dimensions) as a vote in a RANSAC-style consensus that can return "conflict". The learned upgrade (PaddleOCR.js + textline orientation) slots in behind the existing `OcrWord[]` port.

### Cited Findings
- Orientation canonicalisation is how eDOCr2 handles rotation (angle snapping/folding rather than a classifier) — [eDOCr2 source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/javvi51/edocr2/main/edocr2/tools/ocr_pipelines.py). It also matches the aligned-dimension "read from bottom or right" convention — [CCCOnline](https://pressbooks.ccconline.org/bluepringcad/wp-json/pressbooks/v2/chapters/126).
- Rotation classifiers give large OCR gains on rotated input — [Seeing Straight, arXiv 2511.04161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04161v1). An Apache-2.0 browser SDK exposes one — [npm @paddleocr/paddleocr-js](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@paddleocr/paddleocr-js).
- A consensus scale with explicit rejection and conflict outcomes is implemented in PlanMetric — [GitHub](https://github.com/crown-sports/planmetric). Cluster-based scale recovery is in Lv et al. CVPR 2021 — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Lv_Residential_Floor_Plan_Recognition_and_Reconstruction_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf).
- Scale bar = detect + Hough length + OCR labels + user fallback — [MDPI Materials 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12734435/table/array1/). Tick↔label Hungarian matching + RANSAC fit — [arXiv 2608.06062](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.06062). Label OCR is the weak link (F1 75%) — [arXiv 2510.11260](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11260).
- Standard scale series: [ISO 5455 adoption](https://descargas.epconsgtmo.co.cu/Normalizacion%20Actualizadas/Normas%20de%20la%20construccion/Normas%20De%20Dibujo/NC%20ISO%205455-2004%20Dibujo%20T%C3%A9cnico.Escalas.pdf); [LibreCAD imperial table](https://docs.librecad.org/en/latest/appx/scales.html).

### Inferences
**(A) Vertical text read upside-down about 50% of the time** — `src/app/ocr.ts`
1. Replace the `ra.x > rb.x` swap with reading-direction canonicalisation (§3 Inferences): fold θ into [−90°, 90°) and send θ ≥ 90° − 10° to θ − 180°. This should remove the click-order/jitter dependence entirely for aligned-convention sheets.
2. Near vertical (|θ ± 90°| < 20°), dual-read 0°/180°. Choose by NUM_RE parse → no leading zero → confidence → consensus agreement. Add an unrotated midpoint crop for unidirectional sheets.
3. Set `tessedit_char_whitelist` to `0123456789.,:'"-/ xX` (plus `mm`/`m`) on the corridor worker. eDOCr2's digit-only acceptance is the precedent.
4. Later: PaddleOCR.js with `use_textline_orientation: true` as primary engine and Tesseract as fallback (patch-plan Patch 3, now with an official SDK).

**(B) Colon-required ratio misses OCR'd ratios with a dropped colon** — `src/engine/ocr.ts`
1. Accept colon substitutes `[:;.|il!]` and a space ("1 100", "1;100", "1i100") **only** when within one token of a `SCALE`/`@`/`AT` keyword, or followed by `@ A0–A4`.
2. Bare "1NNN" (e.g. "1200") is accepted as 1:NNN only when it directly follows `SCALE`, NNN is in the preferred series {5, 10, 20, 25, 50, 75, 100, 125, 200, 250, 500, 1000, 1250, 2000, 2500, 5000}, and "1NNN" itself is not a preferred ratio. Mark it `low confidence` and require the dimension-consensus vote before auto-applying.
3. Never accept a ratio outside the preferred series without corroboration. Recognise `NTS`/`NOT TO SCALE` and abstain.
4. Prefer the text layer whenever it exists. Born-digital ratios are exact, so the colon problem is OCR-only.

**(C) Imperial and graphic scales**
1. Imperial grammar, normalised after OCR (strip spaces; map ’ ′ → ', ” ″ → "): architectural `(\d+\s?\d*/\d+|\d+(?:\.\d+)?)"=1'-?0?"?` gives ratio = 12 ÷ fraction (1/4 → 48, 3/16 → 64, 1/8 → 96, 3/32 → 128, 1½ → 8). Engineering `1"=(\d+)'(-0")?` gives ratio = 12·N (20' → 240). Also `FULL SIZE` → 1, `HALF SIZE` → 2. Snap to the imperial table. Feed the calibration in inches/feet so the existing unit handling stays consistent.
2. Graphic scale bar tier (new, deterministic): on a "SCALE" keyword hit with no ratio nearby, or on user request, search a band around the keyword for (a) a long horizontal line with ≥3 short perpendicular ticks or an alternating filled/empty block run, and (b) numeric labels in arithmetic progression (0, 1, 2, 5 …) above or below it. Match labels to ticks (Hungarian / nearest-x with gate), fit pt-per-unit by least squares/RANSAC, and require ≥3 inliers. Treat the result as a vote, not an override. Graphic bars stay valid when a sheet is printed reduced, so when bar and ratio disagree by a clean factor (√2, 2, 2√2, i.e. A-series reductions) flag "sheet printed at reduced size" instead of picking one.

**(D) Consensus checks** (new engine function, pure geometry)
1. Each successful dimension calibration stores (segment length pt, value, unit) per page/viewport. Each gives `s_i = value_mm / length_pt`.
2. Votes: title-block ratio (with PDF page size → mm/pt), imperial note, graphic bar, each dimension. Run 1-D RANSAC with a ±2% band. Outcomes: `agreed (n inliers)`, `conflict (list sources + factors)`, `insufficient`. Surface outliers to the user as "this dimension disagrees with 4 others by ×10: check the read".
3. Cheap diagnostics from the factor: ×10 / ×0.1 suggests a unit slip (mm vs cm vs m) or a dropped digit. 1200 vs 200 (×6) is the colon bug. A clean A-series factor means a reduced print.
4. Keep PlanMetric's lesson: consistent wrong evidence still passes, so a viewport boundary (multi-scale sheets, shipped) must partition the votes.

**(E) Vertical words in the text layer** — `src/headless/mcp.ts` (and any app-side text-layer reader): apply the transform-direction fix in §4. Add a regression check with a rotated "1:100" and a vertical "3600" item.

### Gaps
- None of these recommendations has been benchmarked on pdf2takeoff's own sheets. Acceptance should extend patch-plan Patch 3's criterion to vertical dimensions with both click orders, a colon-less "SCALE 1 100", a "1/4" = 1'-0"" sheet and one graphic-bar sheet.
- I found no published error rates for 180° confusion on numeric dimension strings, so the size of the gain from dual-read over canonicalisation alone is unknown until measured.
