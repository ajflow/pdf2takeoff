# Room segmentation, wall/door/opening detection, and door-gap closing for Dynamic Fill

Scope note: this extends `docs/research/ai-literature-review.md` §4 (DeepFloorplan, CubiCasa5K, HEAT, RoomFormer, PolyRoom, MonteFloor, Raster-to-Vector, Deep Vectorization, Adams & Bischof SRG, Ahmed DAS 2012, Pizarro survey are already covered there and are not re-reported except where newer work gives fresh numbers). Method caveat: WebFetch and direct arXiv access were blocked in this session (DNS/proxy refusal), so every figure below comes from search-engine summaries of the primary pages (arXiv abstract/HTML, DOI pages, GitHub READMEs). Numbers were not checked against the full-paper tables; treat them as "as reported in abstract/summary".

Current implementation (read from `src/engine/dynamicfill.ts`): ink = luminance < 150; a per-pixel "closing threshold" array marks every ink-free horizontal or vertical run of length L ≤ 2r that has ink at both ends; the flood fill escalates r through `GAP_HALF = [0, 8, 16, 28, 44]` px only while the fill exceeds `MAX_FILL_FRACTION = 0.4` of the page. The three open problems follow directly from that design: (1) a door between two rooms inside a sealed building never makes the fill leak past 40%, so r stays 0; (2) any short axis-aligned run with ink at both ends gets filled, including the strip between a wall and a parallel bench or worktop line; (3) only axis-aligned runs are ever considered.

## Q1. Learned floor plan parsing (raster): state of the art, 2024–2026 successors, datasets, size, runtime

### Takeaway
The 2024–2026 learned line has moved from per-pixel segmentation to direct polygon or graph output. The current published best on CubiCasa5K is Raster2Seq (SIGGRAPH 2026, Room F1 88.7). For the first time there is a licence-clean training corpus (ResPlan, CC BY 4.0, 17k plans with walls, doors and room polygons), which removes the CC BY-NC blocker the patch plan flagged for a shipped door/opening model. None of these models is browser-sized out of the box. A small opening segmenter trained on ResPlan is the realistic client-side learned tier.

### Inferences
- The fine-grained room/door F1 race (RoomFormer vs Raster2Seq) matters less to pdf2takeoff than two facts: (a) door/window F1 sits near 78% even for SOTA, so a learned door gate will still miss about 1 in 5 openings and needs the geometric tiers as backup; (b) Zhang 2026 shows that a detection readout (heatmaps of junctions and openings) beats sequence decoding on real, large, scan-like plans. Heatmap detection is also the architecture that is easiest to make small and run under ONNX Runtime Web.
- ResPlan (CC BY 4.0) changes Tier B of patch 2. A tiny U-Net or heatmap model predicting "opening/door chord" trained on ResPlan renders is licence-clean, unlike CubiCasa5K or FloorPlanCAD. Caveat: the real-estate-listing provenance needs a legal sanity check before shipping weights.
- FloorplanVLM and SALI-FP confirm the curved/non-Manhattan problem is handled in learned systems only at server scale. Client-side, problem 3 needs the geometric fixes in Q2/Q5.

- No parameter counts or browser/CPU latency were found for Raster2Seq, RoomFormer or FRI-Net. The Raster2Seq 0.52 s figure has no stated hardware.
- MSD's licence was not confirmed in this session.
- I found no 2024–2026 paper that re-benchmarks DeepFloorplan under the same protocol as the new methods.
- I could not verify the PolyRoom 54.1 CubiCasa5K figure against the PolyRoom paper. PolyRoom was designed for point-cloud input, so the low number may reflect a domain mismatch.

## Q2. Classical/geometric door-gap detection: door arcs, facing wall endpoints, narrowest-passage segmentation

### Takeaway
Robotics map segmentation is a mature, directly transferable toolbox for "two rooms joined by an opening": morphological erosion, distance-transform thresholding and Voronoi critical points, plus newer bottleneck decompositions (DUDE). Morphological and distance-transform splitting are the cheapest and nearly as accurate. Voronoi has the best recall on furnished maps. On drawings, the strongest cheap cues are the door swing arc (a quarter circle hinged on a wall end) and pairs of wall endpoints facing each other across a door-width gap.

### Cited Findings
- **Room Segmentation: Survey, Implementation, and Analysis**, Bormann, Jordan, Li, Hampp, Hägele, ICRA 2016. Four open implementations compared on 20 floor plans: morphological (repeated erosion, keep separated regions of valid area, then wavefront fill), distance transform (threshold the distance map at increasing levels and keep the level giving the most valid segments), Voronoi (pruned Voronoi graph, critical points near obstacles become split lines) and feature-based. — [paper PDF (ROS wiki mirror)](https://mirror.umd.edu/roswiki/attachments/ipa_room_segmentation/article.pdf); [Fraunhofer](http://publica.fraunhofer.de/dokumente/N-399711.html); [ipa_room_segmentation docs](https://mirror.umd.edu/roswiki/ipa_room_segmentation.html)
- Bormann: Voronoi achieves the highest recall in the more realistic case with furniture. Morphological and distance-transform methods are the most computationally efficient but may perform poorly in wide open spaces. — [paper](https://mirror.umd.edu/roswiki/attachments/ipa_room_segmentation/article.pdf); [Sharif et al. 2023 summary](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.13798)
- Independent numbers (different maps):
  - On a lidar-map benchmark, Voronoi scored 93.5% recall, 86.6% precision and 2.07 s; morphological scored 94.7% / 91.3% / 1.25 s. — [PMC7919285](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7919285)
  - On 10 cluttered maps, Voronoi scored 71.92% recall / 85.86% precision, morphological 82.3% / 84.98%, and distance 79.79% / 88.34%. All degrade with clutter and noise. — [ROSE², Luperto et al., arXiv:2203.03519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03519)
- **ROSE²** (Luperto et al., 2022): identifies dominant wall directions to segment cluttered or partial maps. It notes Voronoi methods depend on narrow passages that clutter can obscure. — [arXiv:2203.03519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03519)
- **2D Floor Plan Segmentation Based on Down-sampling**, Sharif, Mohan, Suvarna, arXiv:2303.13798 (2023). Iterative Gaussian down-sampling extracts core segments (seeds), then watershed, then three heuristic merge rules. Claims much lower computational and implementation complexity than prior methods. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13798)
- **Occupancy-Grounded Room Segmentation for Hierarchical 3D Scene Graphs**, Cueto Zumaya, Catalano, Peña-Queralta, Bessa, arXiv:2606.13727 (2026). Uses incremental DUDE (dual-space decomposition), which splits free space at natural constrictions such as doorways and narrow passages. The threshold d_thr sets granularity: 1.5 separates room-scale areas, 3.0 merges some neighbours, 6.0 lumps most space together. About 2.5 ms per update. Recovers more room instances at lower precision. Wall-accurate boundaries remain open. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13727)
- Door swing detection, open-source practice:
  - The matchline project's raster detector (NumPy-only) looks for a thin door leaf, a quarter-circle arc, wall-band evidence and a clear opening across 8 orientations. It snaps hinges to wall centres, scores arc fit and dedupes candidates. — [matchline PR #571](https://github.com/anchapin/matchline/pull/571)
  - Its vector variant reads quarter-circle swing arcs bounding wall gaps, matching swing endpoints and hinge radii with 20% tolerances. The authors note that on vector sheets doors are geometry, so no trained weights are needed. — [matchline PR #791](https://github.com/anchapin/matchline/pull/791)
  - These are open-source engineering, not peer-reviewed.
- Hinged-door drawing convention: a straight leaf plus a quarter-circle arc centred on the hinge, with radius equal to the leaf width (so arc radius ≈ opening width). — [Plancio symbols guide](https://plancio.com/en/floor-plans/floor-plan-symbols/); [Plansnapper](https://plansnapper.com/symbols/door)
- Facing-endpoint and collinear gap bridging (open-source practice):
  - One vectorizer snaps a wall's two faces to one line, then bridges collinear gaps up to about 12% of the plan's short side so doorways don't merge rooms. Gaps above about 3.5% are kept as door candidates. Its own notes recommend matching wall endpoints in a graph rather than single collinear gaps, so that skewed walls also join. — [interior-ai PR #112](https://github.com/justuslaw66-max/interior-ai/pull/112); [PR #126](https://github.com/justuslaw66-max/interior-ai/pull/126)
  - The CVPR 2021 *Residential Floor Plan Recognition and Reconstruction* (Lv et al.) detects wall endpoints as heatmaps classified by direction and assembles walls by integer programming. — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Lv_Residential_Floor_Plan_Recognition_and_Reconstruction_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf)
- A 2013 USPTO patent (*Floor plan space detection*, US 9,367,737) uses Hough for long wall lines and door locations to bound and seed room spaces. This is evidence of the commercial lineage, not of performance. — [USPTO 9367737](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9367737)

### Inferences
- For a raster flood fill, the distance-transform family maps onto the existing code with almost no new machinery. Fill at r=0, compute the Euclidean distance transform (EDT) inside the filled mask, and look for necks: pixels whose EDT is a saddle with value ≈ half a door width (0.35–0.5 m at the calibrated scale) and whose two flanking ink contacts are wall ends. Cutting along the neck and keeping the click's component splits two rooms joined by a door even when nothing leaks. This is Bormann's distance segmentation restricted to one region, so it costs O(region pixels) and runs in milliseconds.
- Voronoi/medial-axis splitting is more robust to furniture clutter (Bormann) but more work in JS (skeletonisation plus pruning). It is worth it only if EDT-necking proves noisy.
- Door arcs are the highest-precision cue on architectural drawings because a quarter-circle hinged on a wall end almost never occurs in other linework. Arc radius also gives the gap width for free, which sets the closing length locally instead of via a global ladder.
- Pure bottleneck methods will also cut at legitimate narrow parts of one room (L-shaped rooms, alcoves, kitchen pass-throughs). This matches the "wide open spaces" and over-segmentation caveats in Bormann and the DUDE ablation. Cuts should therefore require corroboration: wall endpoints at both ends of the neck, and ideally an arc, a door tag or two room labels.

### Gaps
- The full Bormann results table (per-method recall/precision/runtime on the 20 maps) could not be retrieved. Only the qualitative ranking and third-party numbers are given above.
- I found no peer-reviewed paper benchmarking door-arc detection specifically as a room-closing step on construction PDFs. The evidence is open-source code plus the learned door classes of DeepFloorplan and CubiCasa.
- *Topology-based Visual Active Room Segmentation* (ICRA 2025, [PDF](https://freeformrobotics.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/ICRA2025_Segmentation.pdf)) and *Semantic Layering in Room Segmentation via LLMs* ([arXiv:2403.12920](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.12920)) surfaced but were not read. Their methods and numbers are unknown.

## Q3. Separating adjacent rooms connected by an opening (door symbols, room labels, bottlenecks)

### Takeaway
Three signals separate merged rooms, and they combine well: (1) an explicit opening detection (arc, door tag, or learned door class) that inserts a virtual wall chord; (2) bottleneck detection in the filled region; (3) room labels or numbers from the PDF text layer as watershed markers. A fill containing two or more room labels is merged by definition. Label-seeded splitting is used in practice, but I found no published evaluation of it.

### Cited Findings
- DeepFloorplan treats doors and windows as a boundary class that gates room segmentation (already in the review). Raster2Seq and RoomFormer output door and window polygons alongside rooms at about 78% F1 on CubiCasa5K. — [Raster2Seq](https://doi.org/10.1145/3799902.3811124)
- The commercial practice of "closing openings by replacing doors/windows with solid lines" (Kreo) is already noted in the review. Togal's help centre describes its room detection as recognising the lines that form room boundaries. It outputs footprint, net, gross and door-opening areas as a starting point for user review. — [Togal help: area & wall detection](https://help.togal.ai/en/articles/15602901-ai-powered-area-and-wall-detection); [Togal button](https://help.togal.ai/en/articles/15602961-the-togal-button-togal-s-automated-takeoff)
- **Vendor claim, unverified:** Togal markets about 98% accuracy from a University of Kansas comparison against estimators. A partner page says 97%. No method or test set is published. — [Togal blog](https://www.togal.ai/blog/ai-blueprint-reading-accuracy); [eTakeoff press release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241030940672/en/eTakeoff-Announces-Availability-of-Cutting-Edge-AI-Takeoff-Solution)
- Ahmed et al. and de las Heras et al. use text segmentation in their room-detection pipelines. A related work proposes post-processing that splits a physical room into sub-regions when several semantic rooms share it. — [Ahmed et al., Automatic Room Detection and Room Labeling (DAS 2012)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/230675743_Automatic_Room_Detection_and_Room_Labeling_from_Architectural_Floor_Plans); [de las Heras et al., IJDAR 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10032-013-0215-2)
- Geometry-seeded watershed is the common room-splitting pipeline: wall mask → EDT → Otsu-thresholded seed cores → watershed → room masks. Text is used afterwards for naming. An open-source tool combines watershed with OCR room labels and notes it works best when labels are clear. — [mageaustralia/FloorPlanAnalyzer](https://github.com/mageaustralia/FloorPlanAnalyzer); [Sharif et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13798)
- A practitioner write-up on door understanding reports that label OCR is messy (abbreviations, typos, compound names) and that fuzzy matching against known room types helped. This is a blog, not peer-reviewed. — [Koleda, Medium](https://andrey-koleda.medium.com/how-i-built-a-computer-vision-system-that-understands-doors-on-floor-plans-ec3dbf0e29c7)
- ResPlan room graphs encode `via_door` edges between rooms, which is direct supervision for "which rooms are separated by which door". — [ResPlan GitHub](https://github.com/m-agour/ResPlan)

### Inferences
- pdf2takeoff has a structural advantage most of the literature lacks: born-digital PDFs carry a text layer, which the manifest already round-trips. Room names and numbers ("BED 2", "G.04", "LIVING 24.5 m²") are free markers with no OCR error. A cheap and robust rule: after a fill, count room-label text items inside the polygon. If there are two or more, the fill merged rooms, so run the splitting tiers (arc chords, neck cut, or marker-controlled watershed seeded at the labels) and return the component containing the click. This turns problem 1 from "never detected" into "detected whenever the plan labels its rooms", which most construction plans do.
- The label count also gives a principled escalation trigger to replace or supplement the 40%-of-page leak trigger. Other triggers: the fill's area exceeds the area written in its own label, or it exceeds a plausible maximum room area at the calibrated scale.
- Door tags (door numbers in circles or hexagons on commercial plans) are another text-layer cue to locate openings without arc detection.

### Gaps
- I found no published accuracy figures for label-seeded room splitting. It is an engineering extrapolation and should be validated on the repo's sample PDFs.
- I could not assess how often real tender drawings omit room labels (e.g., structural or services sheets). This bounds how far the label trigger can be relied on.

## Q4. Avoiding closing furniture/fixture lines: line weight, layers, wall thickness, walls vs fixtures

### Takeaway
The classical literature consistently identifies walls by structure, not single cues: parallel line pairs at a consistent separation (wall thickness), long relative to thickness, aligned with dominant directions, or solid/hatched fills with a characteristic run-length. Thickness alone is unreliable because notations vary. For problem 2 specifically, the fix is to bridge only gaps that continue a wall line collinearly. A run between a wall and a parallel bench is bounded by line sides, not line ends, and can be rejected with a cheap ink-continuation test.

### Cited Findings
- de las Heras et al. note that walls may be drawn as one line of varying width, two or more parallel lines, or hatched patterns, so the old "walls are the thickest lines" assumption limits a method to one notation. — [Unsupervised Wall Detector in Architectural Floor Plans, ICDAR 2013 (PDF)](https://refbase.cvc.uab.cat/files/HFV2013.pdf); [Statistical segmentation and structural recognition for floor plan interpretation, IJDAR 17, 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10032-013-0215-2)
- Their unsupervised wall detector rests on six assumptions: walls are parallel lines, rectangular, longer than thick, in orthogonal directions, with distinct exterior and interior thicknesses, etc. It detects parallel-line candidates in several orientations and ranks them statistically. For solid walls it histograms black-run widths and clusters them, each cluster being a common wall thickness. — [HFV2013 PDF](https://refbase.cvc.uab.cat/files/HFV2013.pdf)
- A follow-up combining structural and statistical strategies "significantly outperforms" the original across notations and shapes. — [Combining structural and statistical strategies (PDF)](https://refbase.cvc.uab.es/files/HVS2013.pdf)
- Ahmed et al. separate thick, medium and thin lines to tell exterior walls, interior walls and other content apart. This is a thickness-class approach that works when the drawing uses line weights consistently. — [summarised in de las Heras HSL2013](https://refbase.cvc.uab.es/files/HSL2013.pdf)
- Multi-unit plans: a UVic thesis addresses wall extraction and room detection for multi-unit architectural plans. — [UVic dspace](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/server/api/core/bitstreams/189b0857-eaf8-49d0-b6b1-11c768e85ce4/content); see also *Recognizing and Reconstructing a Multi-Unit Floor Plan* ([arXiv:2408.01526](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.01526)), not read in detail.
- ROSE² uses dominant wall directions to tell structure from clutter in robot maps. This is the robotics analogue of "furniture lines are not walls". — [arXiv:2203.03519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03519)
- Bormann: furniture clutter degrades morphological and distance methods more than Voronoi. — [Bormann et al. 2016](https://mirror.umd.edu/roswiki/attachments/ipa_room_segmentation/article.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Collinear-continuation test (directly fixes problem 2).** In `closingThresholds`, a horizontal run of length L is currently filled whenever it has ink at both ends. Additionally require that the ink at each end continues along the same axis for at least c·L px or at least the estimated wall length scale (e.g., 1.5× the estimated wall thickness, minimum about 6 px), i.e., the run sits between two line ends.
  - A door gap in a horizontal wall passes: the wall continues left and right of the gap.
  - A vertical run across the strip between a horizontal wall and a parallel bench line fails: above and below the run the ink is only one stroke thick before free space again.
  - This keeps the current O(pixels) single-pass precompute. It needs one extra run-length lookup per gap end, using per-row and per-column ink run lengths computed once.
- **Gap-band thickness test.** A real wall opening, once bridged, forms a band whose perpendicular thickness roughly equals the wall thickness (the same gap length repeats over about T rows). Estimate T once per page with de las Heras's run-length histogram of ink perpendicular runs (the mode of short black runs gives stroke and wall widths). Then reject a bridged band that is much thicker than T, which is what a strip between bench and wall looks like (it repeats over the whole bench length).
- **Vector-layer cues are available and stronger than raster heuristics.** In a born-digital PDF, pdf.js operator lists give each path's stroke width, dash pattern, colour and fill. CAD exports usually draw walls at heavier stroke widths or as filled/hatched polygons, and furniture at thin widths, often in grey. Building the fill's barrier mask from paths at or above a wall-weight percentile when closing gaps (but all ink when bounding at r=0) would stop furniture lines from participating in bridging. This applies Ahmed's line-weight classes, with exact widths instead of estimated ones. Caveat: some plotters flatten all line weights, so keep the raster path as a fallback.
- Fixture lines rarely satisfy both conditions: they seldom end exactly collinear with each other across a door-width gap, and they rarely form parallel pairs at wall separation. Combining the collinear-continuation test and the wall-thickness estimate should remove most false bridges without any learned model.

### Gaps
- I found no 2023–2026 peer-reviewed paper that specifically evaluates gap-closing that is aware of furniture versus walls. The cited methods are 2011–2014 wall-detection work plus robotics clutter studies. The proposed tests are engineering inferences to validate on samples.
- I did not measure how consistently real CAD-to-PDF exports preserve stroke widths. This needs a check on the repo's sample PDFs, e.g., by histogramming `setLineWidth` values from the pdf.js operator list.

## Q5. Recommendations mapped to the three open problems (ranked by effort vs benefit, client-side JS)

### Takeaway
Fix problem 2 first with a collinear-continuation constraint in the existing closing-threshold pass (hours, removes the under-reporting). Then make escalation semantic rather than leak-only: room-label count, area plausibility, plus an always-on door-width closing at the calibrated scale, guarded by the problem 2 fix. Then add door-arc chords and EDT neck-cutting for problem 1. Handle diagonal and curved walls (problem 3) with endpoint-pair bridging, which is orientation-free and also subsumes the axis-aligned closing, plus dominant-angle closing as a cheaper intermediate. Learned opening detection trained on CC BY 4.0 ResPlan is a later tier.

### Cited Findings
- Bridging collinear gaps between wall faces while keeping wider gaps as door candidates, and the recommendation to move to graph endpoint matching for skewed walls, come from open-source vectorizer practice. — [interior-ai PR #112](https://github.com/justuslaw66-max/interior-ai/pull/112)
- Arbitrary-angle linear closing: many digital line orientations must be considered. A simple baseline is to rotate the image, apply an axis-aligned SE, and rotate back. — [GPU linear openings with arbitrary angle, JRTIP](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11554-012-0248-7); [Efficient binary and run-length morphology, arXiv:0712.0121](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0712.0121)
- Path openings and closings use thin structuring elements that need not be straight, so they follow curved structures, with an efficient implementation available. — [Efficient Path Openings and Closings (Hendriks / Talbot & Appleton lineage)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/226729086_Efficient_Path_Openings_and_Closings)
- Dominant wall directions can be estimated robustly from the map (ROSE²). — [arXiv:2203.03519](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03519)
- Distance-transform and morphological room segmentation are the cheapest robotics methods. Voronoi is best under clutter. DUDE runs at about 2.5 ms per update. — [Bormann 2016](https://mirror.umd.edu/roswiki/attachments/ipa_room_segmentation/article.pdf); [arXiv:2606.13727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13727)
- Vector door swings identify door gaps without trained weights. — [matchline PR #791](https://github.com/anchapin/matchline/pull/791)
- ResPlan is CC BY 4.0, with doors, walls, room polygons and `via_door` graphs. ResPlan-FP is CC BY 4.0. — [ResPlan GitHub](https://github.com/m-agour/ResPlan); [arXiv:2608.25608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25608)
- Learned SOTA door/window F1 is about 78% on CubiCasa5K. — [Raster2Seq](https://doi.org/10.1145/3799902.3811124)

### Inferences
Ranked plan. Effort assumes familiarity with `dynamicfill.ts`; benefit is judged against the three open problems.

Notes on combining:
- Ranks 1–3 together are the minimum viable fix set. They are all edits to the existing threshold array and fill loop, with no new data structures beyond per-axis run lengths and the text layer already in the manifest.
- Rank 5 (endpoint pairing) is the principled long-term replacement for axis-aligned closing. Pairing line ends, not arbitrary ink, inherently avoids the bench-strip failure and is orientation-free. Curved walls get a straight chord across the opening, and the chord-vs-arc area error over a 0.9 m opening is small relative to room area.
- Ranks 4 and 6 add corroboration for each other. Cut a neck automatically only when it has wall ends at both sides and either an arc, two labels on opposite sides, or a door tag nearby. Otherwise surface it as a suggested split, to avoid cutting L-shaped rooms at their elbows.
- Keep the existing area-preserving property: every bridge adds pixels to the barrier only along the gap and never dilates walls, so traced polygons keep true wall faces.

### Gaps
- None of the ranked items has been benchmarked on pdf2takeoff's own sample drawings. Effort and benefit are engineering estimates, and a small labelled set of click-to-area cases (rooms with internal doors, bench strips, a diagonal wing, a curved wall) is needed to rank them empirically.
- I found no peer-reviewed evaluation of endpoint-pair bridging or label-seeded splitting for area takeoff accuracy specifically. The evidence is robotics segmentation results plus open-source practice.
- The real-estate provenance of ResPlan needs a licensing review before shipping weights trained on it.
